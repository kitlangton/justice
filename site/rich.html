“Good typography,” someone once said, is invisible. But a paragraph is rarely just plain text. It has links that carry the reader from one thought to another, even when they run across several lines, quiet emphasis, and the occasional inline code. Justice keeps the details: nested bold links, small capitals, and a change of color. Even typography can change style inside a word. Well-considered line breaks give these different voices the same measured rhythm, without separating Mr. Smith or losing their meaning.

Text is measured once after the font loads. Changing the width reuses those measurements. Links remain ordinary links; emphasis remains ordinary HTML.
